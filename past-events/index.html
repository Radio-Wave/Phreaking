Past Events archive documenting talks, workshops, screenings, and performances organised by Phreaking Collective between 2025 and 2026. Events were presented through two exhibitions: BitRot (2025) and Does Cloud Compute (ever) Precipitate? (2026), both held in London. Participants and contributors include Lucia Rebolino, Terence Broad, Robin Leverton, Dian Joy, Erin Robinson, Anthony Frisby, Kat Macdonald, Cranberry Lemonade, Matt Hill, Margot McEwen, Melissa Li Yunzhi, Claire McMichael, Lagtime Seedling, Deposit Silk, Yiding Zhang, Orangespy, That Sounds Exciting!, and Algorave London. Films were screened by Lucy Ellis, Autojektor, Atay Ilgun, Maggie Maggot, Erinne Huston, Dana Fox, Shannon Lu, Verona Gryshchuk, Max Colson, Natalie Maximova, Isaac Ward, Tilly Hawkins, Ned Caderni, Iman Javaid, Julian Law, Ngo Chun Phoenix Tse, Mati Granica, and Yidi Chen. Topics span digital art, computational art, AI art, live coding, algorithmic art, experimental media, creative coding, internet art, new media art, media archaeology, internet infrastructure, and critical AI.

Talk

Designing Futures Through Absence of Data

Tiziana Alocci — 2026

Data artist and UAL Associate Lecturer Tiziana Alocci asked what happens to everything digital systems cannot measure as they optimise for speed, extraction, prediction, and surveillance, arguing that the absence of data often matters more than what gets recorded. Drawing on her projects transforming invisible data — soundscapes, personal testimonies, found online data, electromagnetic fields — into data-driven installations, she explored how alternative data collection methods can challenge dominant technological narratives and surface what contemporary systems routinely overlook, turning seen and unseen data alike into something tangible and perceivable.

Talk

An I Without a Face

Marcus Round — 2026

Marcus Round moved between magic trick, technical demonstration, and personal essay to ask what we mean when we say "I" — in a sentence, in a chatbot, in a body, in grief. Expanded from a shorter work first presented with tiat at the Internet Archive, the lecture searched for the human voice in a world of endlessly generated language, guiding the audience through a group tarot card game session to explore chatbot sentience, meaning-making, and grief.

Talk

Spiral: the shape of cultural temporality & protocol-art

Atay Ilgun — 2026

Atay Ilgun, a London-based artist, creative technologist, and curator whose work bridges AI, web3, event curation, and experimental art, drew on his practice across early AI, crypto, and protocol art to think through cultural time and the hype cycle, using the spiral as a form that reads emergence and obsolescence together. He proposed network and protocol art as work that operates from inside its own infrastructure, holding its prior states forward as material rather than breaking from market-dependent digital culture — a reality hack from within it. The talk moved through Otherness and the ./spiral exhibition, co-curated with jonooo on SuperRare, alongside soft world, his curatorial studio for protocol art, examining hypercycles, crypto, and alternative paths toward a quieter, more durable version of what digital culture could still become.

Yunzhi Melissa Li, a Chengdu-born, London-based computational artist whose practice spans interactive installations, web-based experiments, and immersive 3D environments, delivered a 30-minute narrated performance lecture navigating a custom-built 3D world. Talk

Interactive digital art: Tools for collective feeling and becoming

Olivia Ema — 2026

Olivia Ema, a lecturer at University of the Arts London, digital artist, and founder of a creative technology studio, spoke as a Black woman working in creative technology about how identity and intuition shape the systems and worlds she builds. She walked through how digital systems built in software like Touchdesigner could hold presence, respond to a room, and shift something in people almost without them noticing, drawing on her own projects — an interactive floor installation reflecting on grief, and large-scale installations across London from Outernet to Piccadilly. The talk moved through both the technical process behind the work and what was actually driving it underneath, staying grounded in theory and critical awareness throughout. Ringtone Workshop

Kat McDonald — 2026

Kat Macdonald, a London-based sound and performance artist exploring collective practice, desire, and public space through technology and performance, led participants through the history of ringtone customization and the politics of sound in the commons. Using RTTTL, the Ring Tone Text Transfer Language developed by Nokia in 1996 to make and share ringtones between early mobile phones, participants made and shared their own custom ringtones, centering ideas around the politics of sound in public space.

Encrypted Embroidery

Sophie Nadel — 2026

Sophie Nadel led a hands-on workshop teaching participants how to translate an address, password, or secret phrase into a hidden code and embroider it onto their belongings, drawing on her design system supported by the Data Reclamation Handbook. The practice encouraged storing information offline, away from prying data collection, while offering a slow, meditative approach to data sovereignty that recentred embroidery as a craft historically dismissed as "women's work." Participants brought their own items to embroider, with needles, scissors, and materials provided.

Performance Night

Slow Impulse

Phreaking Collective — 2026

A six act experimental performance night delving into sounds from unusual sources, emanating throughout a large school hall to close off our fesival <<Can We Start Again?

[Event Over]

↗View 6 performances

Slow Impulse, an experimental sound night, was the final closing event of our festival Can We Start Again? and moved through six sets that each pulled sound from unexpected places. Ellis Berwick and Willow Swan opened with their sculptural, industrial instruments, setting a tone that was as much about materials as music. The London Community Laptop Orchestra followed, creating a communal space within the stage for people to play and interact with their laptops and phones before settling into a slower stretch of harmonizing healing frequencies. Genia Isachenko then took over with a rig of amplifiers, speakers and an old radio, weaving vocals through static and interference. Lagtime Seedling and Rosemary Moss played a double youtube duet across two projectors on either side of the large school hall. Conscript Theatre's Elia Fielden and Emile Belli reworked fragments of 'Untitled Robot Story,' folding writing about military machinery, memory and love into sound. Finally, the night closed with a full concerto of phones and alarms, guitars, cello and piano alongside ringtones and clocks.

Performance 1

Willow Swan + Ellis Berwick

Willow Swan and Ellis Berwick are an artist duo working across sound, sculpture, and performance. Rooted in a shared interest in repurposing industrial materials into improvised instruments, their collaborative practice transforms functional structures into resonant, site-responsive sonic forms.

Performance 2

The London Community Laptop Orchestra

The London community laptop orchestra is a community run laptop orchestra making sounds in a variety of unusual places.

Performance 3

Genia Isachenko

Genia Isachenko is a sound artist and vocalist working with obsolete tech and experimental performance. Presenting a live surround set using discarded amplifiers and speakers to create their own diffusion array, and exploring the timbral differences of radio, interference and voice through song.

Performance 4

Lagtime Seedling + Rosemary Moss

Lagtime Seedling creates audio-visual compositions on their laptop using live web browser windows and overlapping youtube video tabs, while Rosemary Moss draws from their musical background to create intimate sound worlds to deconstruct and index experience. While they independently approach sound in different ways, through collaboration, they settle in a new and unexplored in-between zone.

Performance 5

Elia Fielden & Emile Belli // Conscript Theatre

Conscript Theatre is a hyper-material performance company bringing creative technology onstage alongside new writing, experimental performance, and alternative dramaturgy. For this performance, sound artists Elia Fielden and Emile Belli present fragments from Conscript's new show, 'Untitled Robot Story', a piece about robotic military machinery, memory, and love.

Performance 6

A Concerto for Strings & Alarms

“Concerto for Strings & Alarms” is a musical concerto composed and played for a variety of alarms and ringtones, featuring two guitarists, a cellist, a pianist, alongside phones, clocks, and interference.

Performance

Lagtime Seedling

Lagtime Seedling — 2026
Part of Does Cloud Compute (ever) Precipitate?

Lagtime Seedling scores and performs audio-visual compositions made entirely within web browser windows using a large number of live youtube.com video tabs. Through a meticulously choreographed way of using their laptop, they create narrative and musical journeys through strange and overlooked corners of the web. Transparency and accessibility is central to their practice: Lagtime Seedling doesn't use any traditional music software. Instead, all their sound comes from youtube tabs and a piano keyboard on a separate virtualpiano.net browser window. They interact with their browser as anyone could, using keyboard shortcuts to navigate many tabs and windows quickly, and interact with videos through youtube's default hotkeys. The sound and visuals of lagtime's compositions, being pulled straight from youtube, carry on a certain algorithmic qualia. Full of ASMR, slime squishing, nail scratching, and soft spoken words, their compositions reflect the overstimulating experience of being online. Through a carefully arranged score, lagtime recontextualises online content, extracting overlooked emotion embedded within it.
